Your head is for the work.
The box holds the rest.

A gap with nothing in front of you turns into a scroll, or the next episode. An idea in the middle of study has nowhere to sit, so it tugs at you, or you lose it an hour later. You put both in the box. mm shows one thing.

You’re about to study. Put it in the box, so your head can do the studying.

mmone thing at a time
stack on top, until it’s done
queue the line of work
quick the nag, off the line

A gap

You finish a problem, or a build is running, and for a minute nothing is in front of you. A feed will take that minute. It starts without asking you to decide.

Put the next piece of work in the queue before you need it. When the gap opens, type mm. The next thing is already there.

An idea, while you are in it

You are studying, or deep in the work. An idea arrives. Later, another thing you mean to do after this. Keep it in your head and it pulls. Go do it and the work is gone.

mm add "write this up later" lines it up behind the work you are in. You stay on that work. When you finish, the idea is next. A tiny one, mm add -q "email Sam", waits off the line until you want it.

What goes where

  • Queue the work you meant to do. First in, first done. mm add "CS302"
  • Stack something just became more important. Do it, then you are back. mm add -p "prod is down"
  • Quick a small thing that would nag you or clutter the line. mm add -q "email Sam"

You can keep about four things in mind, then the rest spills (Cowan). Writing a task down is how people already free attention (Risko and Gilbert). Leaving one unfinished leaves part of you on it (Leroy), and getting back after a real interruption takes a long time, on the order of twenty minutes in Gloria Mark’s office studies. The box is that place, written down. The old claim that you remember unfinished tasks better has not held up. mm does not depend on it.

A todo list stores the work and then asks you to choose. A calendar decides when. Notes and tabs can hold an idea, and still don’t tell you what is next or put you back. mm shows one thing, and it never takes the clock. Tomorrow the box is empty unless you add the work again.

Install

Python 3.11 or newer. Nothing else to install.

git clone https://github.com/Toseef-Ahmad/mm.git
cd mm && ./install.sh

That puts mm on your path, and man mm beside it. The box is one file, ~/.mm/state.json.

If this happens

  • A gap opened mm
  • An idea arrived mm add "write this up later"
  • You finished mm done
  • You’re stuck mm block "waiting"
  • Park it, still unfinished mm suspend
  • Time for the small things mm flush-quick
  • See the whole box mm status
  • Take it back mm undo
